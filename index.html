Fuse troubleshooting

Use a selected position’s “Likely effect” as a clue. A fuse listing identifies the circuit it protects; it does not prove the fuse is the cause of a symptom.

1 · Match the symptomSearch for the affected feature or a symptom, then select a matching position. A failed bulb, motor, switch, wiring, or module can cause the same symptom.
2 · Switch off firstTurn the vehicle and affected accessories off before inspecting a fuse. Use the owner’s manual and the diagram on your vehicle’s fuse-block cover to locate it.
3 · Inspect carefullyLook for a broken or melted silver band. Replace a bad fuse only with one of the identical size and rating; never bridge it or install a higher-rated fuse.
4 · If it opens againStop replacing it and have the circuit checked. A repeat failure can point to an electrical fault that a new fuse will not fix.

Safety: If braking, steering, engine temperature, required lights, or wipers are affected, stop somewhere safe and get the vehicle checked before continuing. If an airbag or passenger-sensing warning appears, arrange service; do not probe restraint-system wiring.
